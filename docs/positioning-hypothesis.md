# Positioning Critique And Hypothesis

## What Is Generic Or Risky In The Current Positioning

1. "AI, agents, automation" can quickly sound like a category list instead of a clear commercial proposition.
2. "Rethinking work" is directionally strong but too broad without a narrow operating context.
3. "Strategy to prototype" is good, but many consultants claim this without showing delivery constraints, risk boundaries, or proof mechanics.
4. "AI-native operating model" and "agentic workflow" are useful technical labels, but many business buyers will not parse them.
5. "Can identify where AI is useful" is credible only if paired with explicit non-use criteria (where standard automation or human ownership should remain).
6. "Understands systems deeply" is currently asserted, not evidenced.
7. The message risks sounding like advisory-only work unless implementation artifacts are foregrounded.

## Terms To Translate For Non-Technical Buyers

- Agentic workflow -> Workflow where software can make bounded decisions and execute tasks, with human checks where needed.
- Decision automation -> Clear rules for when software decides, when humans approve, and how outcomes are audited.
- AI-native operating model -> Work designed around what intelligent software can reliably handle now, not what teams did historically.

## Claims That Need Tightening

1. Avoid broad implied mastery. Use "currently exploring" and "building practical prototypes" instead of "leading" or "transforming" claims.
2. Avoid implied case-study outcomes without published evidence.
3. Make speed claims concrete by describing scope: from ambiguous process to tested prototype workflow, not full enterprise rollout.

## Sharper Differentiation Opportunities

1. Explicitly position around expensive operational friction, not "AI adoption".
2. Show a default posture of skepticism: AI is one tool, not the answer to every process.
3. Emphasize the uncommon combination: former business owner plus software systems builder plus rapid prototyper.
4. State decision boundaries clearly: what should be automated, what should remain human, and why.
5. Introduce a visible "Lab" trajectory to demonstrate thinking through working artifacts over time.

## Positioning Hypothesis (Working)

Dave helps organizations redesign high-friction workflows where information, decisions, and handoffs are slowing delivery or operations.

He works between commercial diagnosis and technical implementation:

- define the operational problem in business terms
- map decisions, context, and responsibility boundaries
- identify where AI is useful and where it is not
- build a working prototype that can be tested with real users and data
- decide whether to invest further based on evidence

This is not AI theater or transformation-program consulting. It is practical prototype engineering for decision-heavy workflows.

## Messaging Constraints For The Site

1. Lead with business problem and outcome, not technology labels.
2. Use plain language first; introduce technical language second.
3. Keep claims measurable and bounded.
4. Maintain a visible build-first orientation.
5. Make the CTA a conversation about a specific process, not a sales funnel.

## Candidate Headline Directions

1. Find the work AI should be doing. Then prove it.
2. From messy workflow to working prototype.
3. AI ideas are easy. Useful systems are harder.
4. Redesign the work. Test the system.
5. Turn operational bottlenecks into testable software.
6. Decide what to automate. Build what matters.
7. Strategy is cheap. Working prototypes are evidence.

## Chosen Headline Direction

Find the work AI should be doing. Then prove it.

Why this direction:

- concrete and commercially legible
- implies discernment (not maximum AI)
- implies implementation and verification, not slides
- supports both non-technical and technical audiences
